Why ERAF Predicts Permits: The Math Behind the 6-Month Lead

By LSCI Analytics Team | July Data Release

Our June analysis flagged a correlation. July's model confirms causation. The Educational Revenue Augmentation Fund (ERAF) shift is not a fiscal footnote—it is a direct, mechanical predictor of planning department capacity with a consistent six-month lead on permit velocity.

The mechanism is brutally simple. Under California law, ERAF automatically diverts a fixed percentage of every dollar of new property tax growth away from cities and counties to backfill schools.

ERAF Diversion = 0.218 × AV Growth

21.8% of all new Assessed Value is diverted before cities budget it

Statewide in FY24-25, that equaled $967 million diverted from LA County municipalities. At an all-in cost of $145,000 per planner (salary, benefits, PERS), that's 6,669 planner-months—or the permanent equivalent of 175 full-time planners removed from the system before a single budget hearing.

Why the 6-month lead? City budgets are adopted in June. The LA County Auditor-Controller finalizes the actual ERAF takeaway the following January. Cities budget conservatively in Q3, freezing discretionary hires (planners, inspectors) immediately. That hiring freeze hits permit desks in Q4—exactly six months later. Our DFP model now captures this lag directly.
